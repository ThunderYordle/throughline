# Throughline: from the draft to a game plan, and back in review

Working name: **Throughline** (see Open questions).
Pitch: **"Did we play our draft?"**
Target: Cloud9 x JetBrains "Sky's the Limit" 2026, Category 2 (LoL), "Macro Scouting & Draft Management System", as an entrant's own coaching innovation.
Deadline: January 12, 2027, 11:00 am Pacific Time.
Judging: January 27 to February 3, 2027.
Winners announced: on or around February 8, 2027.
Prizes in the category: $6,000 plus a GDC 2027 trip for the winner, $2,000 for the runner-up; a separate $1,000 bonus prize for the best AI-powered workflow story.

## Problem

A draft is a set of promises.
A coach drafts an early-game comp because it should take the first two dragons, or a scaling comp because it should trade objectives and survive until 25 minutes.
Those promises live in the coach's head and in a pre-game talk; nothing writes them down in a form the team can check.

After the game, review starts from the VOD, not from the plan.
Analysts scrub for the fights and the throws, and whether the team actually played the comp it drafted is rarely asked directly.
The draft and the macro review are two separate conversations, with two separate tools or, more often, a spreadsheet.

## Goal

One loop, in one tool:

1. **Plan**: enter a finished draft and get the game plan for that matchup, as a short list of checkable items ("contest first dragon with 3+ by 4:40", "avoid even fights from 14:00 to 20:00, their window"), each backed by numbers from pro games.
2. **Review**: pick the game that was played and get, for each plan item, whether the team followed it, with the evidence and a replay clip; plus the macro moments the plan did not cover.

A coach should read the plan in under a minute before the game and walk out of the review knowing which promises the team kept.

## Non-goals

- Draft recommendations: which champion to pick or ban. Throughline starts when the draft is done.
- Opponent scouting reports, champion pools, Fearless tracking, pick priorities.
- Player ratings, recruitment, SoloQ.
- Accounts, teams and permissions. Plans and reviews are saved behind unguessable links.
- Text generated by a language model. Every line is a template filled from counted data, with its sample size.
- Our own team's scrims in the hackathon build: the hackathon's GRID data is official pro games (see Data).

## Users and the core flow

The user is a League of Legends coach or analyst.

1. **Draft**: enter both teams' champions by role and the sides, or open a GRID game to prefill its draft.
2. **Plan**: Throughline builds the plan for that draft. The coach removes, edits or adds items and saves it. The plan gets a link and a Discord export.
3. **Game**: the game is played.
4. **Review**: open the review from the plan and pick the GRID game (offered automatically when a game with the same ten champions exists). Throughline checks every plan item and detects the macro moments.
5. **Debrief**: the coach confirms or dismisses each verdict and moment, adds a note, and shares the review link with the players.

For the hackathon, both halves run on pro games: pick any game in the dataset, see the plan its draft implies (built only from games played before it), then see whether the team played it.

## Part 1: the Plan

### The knowledge base

Built offline from every game in the dataset, per patch window (the game's patch and the two before it, falling back to the whole dataset when samples are thin):

- **Champion tempo curves**: per champion and role, the team's gold difference at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 minutes against the league average for the role, shrunk toward zero by sample size (empirical Bayes, so a champion with 4 games barely moves the curve).
- **Lane matchups**: gold and CS difference at 10 and 14 minutes for a champion pair in a lane, when there are 5+ games; otherwise each champion's own lane curve.
- **Spike timings**: the median time each champion reaches level 6 and completes its first and second legendary item, with the gold-difference change in the 3 minutes after each.
- **Objective tendencies**: per champion, how much its team's first-dragon, grubs, herald and baron rates differ from the league, shrunk the same way.

Each number carries the games behind it, and every plan line shows that count.

### From the draft to tempo

- Sum the five tempo curves per team, and add the matchup term for each lane that has one.
- The difference of the two team curves is the **draft tempo**: who the draft favours at each minute.
- A **window** is a stretch where the draft tempo is clearly on one side (beyond a threshold, named in `plan/constants.py`), labelled "our window" or "their window".
- Each lane gets a **priority** call (ours, theirs, even) from its 10-minute matchup or champion terms.

### Plan items

A plan is 4 to 8 items, each a typed, checkable claim with a time range and an expectation.
Typed items are what lets the review check them.

| Type | Example | Built from | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `objective` | "Contest first dragon (5:00): 3+ in bot river by 4:40, 2+ wards there by 4:30." | Lane priority near the pit, draft tempo at spawn, objective tendencies | Positions and wards before spawn, who took it |
| `concede` | "Give first grubs; trade for plates mid or bot." | Their lane priority near the pit, their window | Our players' distance from the pit at spawn, what we took meanwhile |
| `lane` | "Bot has priority to 8:00: play for plates." | Lane matchup | Plates, wave position and gold difference in that lane to the time |
| `window` | "Our window 16:00-22:00: force fights and objectives." / "Their window 12:00-18:00: avoid even fights." | Draft tempo | Fights started in the range, their numbers and results |
| `spike` | "Their Ahri hits level 6 near 6:20: no mid trades without jungle near." | Spike timings | Deaths in that lane in the 2 minutes after the enemy spike |
| `side` | "Jungle plays bot side to 6:00." | Lane priorities | Jungler's share of time per map half |

Rules for the generator:

- Items are ranked by how strong the signal is and how many games stand behind it; the top 4 to 8 make the plan.
- An item with fewer games behind it than its guard is never generated.
- The coach can edit an item's numbers and text or add one of the six types by hand; the review checks hand-made items the same way.

### The Plan page

- The draft across the top: both teams, sides, roles.
- The tempo chart: draft tempo from 0 to 30 minutes, windows shaded, objective spawns and spike timings marked.
- The plan items as cards, each with its evidence ("Ahri reaches 6 at a median 6:18, 212 games") and an edit control.
- Export: plain text for Discord, the items as a checklist.

## Part 2: the Review

### Plan check

For each plan item, a verdict: **Followed**, **Partly**, **Not followed** or **Not applicable** (for example, a dragon item when the game ended before it or the dragon was already taken).

- Each verdict states the numbers it read ("2 of 5 in bot river at 4:40, 1 ward; dragon taken by them at 5:21").
- Each verdict opens the replay at the moment that decided it.
- The header sums it up: "Plan followed 4 of 6 (2 partly)".

### Macro moments

Moments the plan did not cover, detected from the game alone:

| Moment | Detected when | Verdict reads |
|---|---|---|
| Objective setup | Every objective spawn: arrival time of each player within the pit area, wards placed there in the 90 seconds before | Arrived first, grouped late, no vision, outnumbered |
| Unused advantage | 2+ enemy deaths or 3+ enemy recalls, then 60 seconds with no objective, structure, plate or vision gain | Wasted tempo, or converted (with what) |
| Cross-map trade | Objectives or structures taken by both teams within 45 seconds on opposite halves | Gold value of each side, who came out ahead |
| Caught before an objective | A death with no ally within 2,000 units in the 90 seconds before an objective spawn, after 14:00 | The objective's outcome after it |
| Gold swing | Team gold difference moves 1,500+ within 60 seconds | The events inside the swing, the first one marked as the trigger |

Each moment has a time, a type, a verdict (good, bad, neutral), its numbers and a clip from 15 seconds before to 10 seconds after.
A game yields 8 to 15 moments; when detectors find more, the largest gold impact wins.
Thresholds are named constants in the detector modules and documented in `docs/methodology.md`.

### The Review page

- A timeline of the game with the gold difference, the plan items' time ranges and the moments as markers.
- The plan check, then the moments, as one list in time order, filterable by plan item, moment type and verdict.
- The replay beside the list: picking an entry plays its clip.
- For each entry: Confirm, Dismiss and a note. The review link opens read-only for players.

## Replay (shared by both parts)

A minimap player for one game:

- Champion icons with health rings, wards with their lifetimes, structures, objectives with spawn timers, kills where they happened.
- A scrubber with markers for kills, objectives and the review's moments; speeds 1x to 20x; loops a clip.
- A short trail behind each champion (last 10 seconds), so rotations read at a glance.
- Opened by URL with a game id, a start and an end, so every link in the app is just a URL.

No minion waves: the feed has no minion positions.
Lane state for `lane` items is read from plates, structures and the laners' positions.

## Validation: does following the plan matter?

The methodology write-up answers this with the whole dataset:

1. For every game, build both teams' plans from games played **before** it only (a strict time split, no leakage).
2. Check every plan item against the game.
3. Report the win rate by plan adherence (quarters of "share of items followed") and by item type.

Following a plan is confounded with already winning: a team ahead finds every item easier.
To limit that, the item-level analysis compares teams that followed and did not follow an item **at equal gold difference at the item's start** (bucketed), and the write-up states the remaining limits plainly.
Whatever the result, it is reported; a weak or negative result for an item type is a finding about the generator, and the generator is tuned on a held-out earlier split, never on the reported one.

The headline number goes in the video if it holds up.

## Data

### GRID access

The hackathon gives participants access to official LoL esports data from GRID through an application form.
Throughline uses:

- **Central Data** (GraphQL): tournaments, series, teams, players.
- **Series State**: sides, roles, champions, results per game.
- **File Download**: the `events` JSONL per game.

Events the design needs, each to verify against the hackathon data in week 1:

| Need | Event | Fallback if missing |
|---|---|---|
| Positions | per-player positions in the stats updates | None: the review cannot work without it |
| Wards | `ward_placed`, `ward_killed` | Drop ward checks from `objective` items |
| Kills, objectives, structures, plates | kill, epic monster, building, turret plate events | Plates: drop them from `lane` checks |
| Levels | level-up events or level in stats updates | Drop level-6 `spike` items |
| Items | item purchase events or inventory in stats updates | Drop item `spike` items |
| Gold | total gold per player in stats updates | None: tempo curves need it |

### Pipeline

```
ingest CLI
  ├─ list series for the chosen tournaments (Central Data)
  ├─ per game: download events JSONL (cached on disk, gzipped)
  └─ parse once into compact tables
        games, players, positions (1 Hz), gold (per 10 s), levels, items,
        wards, kills, objectives, structures, plates
build CLI
  ├─ knowledge base per patch window (curves, matchups, spikes, objective tendencies)
  ├─ per game: fights, objective setups, moments
  └─ validation: plans from prior games, checks, adherence tables
API
  ├─ plan: knowledge base lookups + generator (milliseconds)
  └─ review: per-game features + checkers (under a second; cached)
```

### Storage

- **DuckDB** for the analytics: one file, rebuilt by the CLIs, read-only to the API.
- **SQLite** for the app's own records: plans, reviews, confirmations and notes.

The split keeps user data out of a file that is rebuilt, and both run from a fresh clone with no server.
GRID data is not committed to the public repository; the README explains how to ingest with one's own GRID key, and the hosted demo runs on data ingested on the server.

### Dataset

The leagues and splits the hackathon key covers, most recent first.
Target: 600+ games, enough for champion curves on the current patches and for the validation's held-out split.

## Architecture

- **Backend**: Python 3.12, FastAPI, DuckDB, SQLite, Pydantic models for every payload. Analysis is pure functions over per-game data; I/O lives only in the CLIs and the API layer.
  - `backend/throughline/grid/`: GRID clients and the events parser.
  - `backend/throughline/game/`: per-game features (positions, fights, objective setups, lane state).
  - `backend/throughline/knowledge/`: curves, matchups, spikes, shrinkage.
  - `backend/throughline/plan/`: item types, the generator, `constants.py`.
  - `backend/throughline/review/`: one checker per item type, one detector per moment type.
  - `backend/throughline/validation/`: the time-split backtest.
  - `backend/throughline/api/`: routes.
- **Frontend**: React 18, Vite, TypeScript, Tanstack Query, Tailwind.
  - `frontend/src/draft/`, `plan/`, `review/`, `replay/`, `map/` (coordinate transforms and the canvas layer).
- **API**:
  - `GET /champions`, `GET /games?q=&tournament=`
  - `POST /plans` (a draft in, a plan out, saved), `GET /plans/{id}`, `PATCH /plans/{id}`
  - `POST /reviews` (a plan and a game), `GET /reviews/{id}`, `PATCH /reviews/{id}/entries/{entryId}`
  - `GET /games/{id}/replay`
  - `GET /validation`
- **Hosting**: one container (the API serving the built frontend) with both database files, on Railway or Fly, at a public URL with no login.

## Testing

- Parser: fixture event files for 3 games (a blue win, a red win, a short game), asserting counts of kills, wards, objectives, structures and plates against the end state.
- Knowledge base: shrinkage and curve sums on synthetic data with known answers.
- Plan: the generator on fixed knowledge-base fixtures; each item type's guard, ranking and text.
- Review: each checker and detector on small synthetic games (five players walking to a pit at known times, a gold swing of known size) plus snapshot tests on the fixture games.
- Hand checks: for 5 games, compare every verdict and moment with the VOD, and log them in BUILD_LOG.
- Validation: a test that the time split never reads a game on or after the reviewed one.
- Frontend: Vitest for coordinate transforms, the replay clock and the tempo chart's scales; a Playwright test that enters a draft, saves the plan, opens the review and plays one clip.
- CI on GitHub Actions: lint (ruff, eslint), type checks (mypy, tsc), tests, production build.

|---|---|
